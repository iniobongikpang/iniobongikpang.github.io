September 2019

OVC Household Data Validation & Supportive Supervision
Project LGAs - Akwa Ibom State, Nigeria

As part of the routine MEAL activities to ensure data quality, I designed and led a combined household-level data validation and supportive supervision exercise across all OVC implementation sites in Akwa Ibom State as the MEAL Officer on the LOPIN-3 project, running from 8–22 September 2019.

Data Validation & Verification:The exercise involved validating OVC service delivery at household level, covering a 5% sample of all households served within the reporting period across all implementation sites. The validation assessed the consistency of data between physical case folders and entries in the National OVC Management Information System (NOMIS), verified that Community Volunteers (CVs) had established meaningful relationships with enrolled beneficiaries in line with each household's care plan, and confirmed the frequency and quality of services provided. For households where discrepancies or concerns were identified, findings were used to directly inform corrective action.

Supportive Supervision: Alongside the household visits, I led supportive supervision sessions for all LOPIN-3 Civil Society Organisation (CSO) partners, with targeted hands-on mentoring and coaching for M&E Officers and data clerks. This follow-up to a recently concluded M&E performance review meeting was designed to bridge identified gaps and sustain improvements in data reporting quality, with the goal of reaching 100% service provision accuracy across all sites.

To ensure a holistic approach, the exercise was coordinated across a multidisciplinary team including the DME, M&E Advisor, State Team Lead, Case Managers, Community Volunteers, CSO project teams, and drivers — ensuring comprehensive coverage of both field-level and office-based data quality processes across Akwa Ibom State.

September 2019

Capacity Building on NOMIS, Data Use & DQA Processes — Akwa Ibom State
State Government and CSO Implementing Partners.

Continuous Capacity Building Approaches were ultimate to ensure improved Data Qaulity and the evolving MIS for data management. As MEAL Officer for Akwa Ibom State on the HIFASS LOPIN-3 project, I designed and facilitated a structured mentoring and capacity-building session for OVC Desk Officers at state and LGA levels, CSO M&E Officers, and data clerks on 3 September 2019 at K-Orbit Hotel, Ewet Housing, Uyo, Akwa Ibom State.

The session was designed to address critical gaps in data management and reporting across the OVC programme's implementing partners and government counterparts. Given the dynamic nature of the OVC programme — with regularly updated M&E tools, indicators, and reporting requirements — the activity focused on building practical, applied skills across four core areas: NOMIS overview, report generation and data upload; OVC and PASS indicator datasets and Key Performance Indicators (KPIs); internal Data Quality Assessment (DQA) processes and modalities in line with national standards; and adherence to Standard Operating Protocols (SOPs) for data collection, entry, analysis, and use.

The session combined presentations, practical hands-on exercises, and group brainstorming to ensure participants could immediately apply their learning. This approach was deliberately chosen to reduce the cycle of back-and-forth corrections in data reporting and strengthen participants' independent capacity to manage programme data accurately. Participants included the Senior Leadership Team, M&E Advisor, Akwa Ibom State M&E and Programme Units, CSO programme and M&E Officers, data clerks, Case Managers, Akwa Ibom State Government OVC Desk Officers, and representatives from key MDAs including the Ministry of Women Affairs (MoWA), SACA, and SAPC — reinforcing a whole-of-system approach to M&E strengthening across state, LGA, and CSO levels.

Overall, the activity strengthened M&E systems at multiple levels simultaneously, improved data use for informed decision-making, and fostered greater synergy and coordination among personnel across organisational levels — directly contributing to improved data quality and accountability in OVC service delivery across Akwa Ibom State.

August to September 2019

Monthly OVC Service Validation & Verification at Household Level
Project Communities and LGAs — Akwa Ibom State.

To validate provision of services to enrolled households, traingulating with reported data on both primary and secondary data sources, I designed and led a monthly data validation and verification exercise conducted from 30 August to 5 September 2019 across all OVC implementation sites as the MEAL Officer on the HIFASS LOPIN-3 project in Akwa Ibom State.

The exercise targeted a 5% random sample of all households served within the reporting month across every implementation site, as reported by the Civil Society Organisation (CSO) partners. Its core purpose was to validate data integrity between physical case folders and NOMIS entries, verify that Community Volunteers (CVs) had established meaningful, consistent relationships with enrolled beneficiaries in line with each household's care plan, and confirm that the frequency and quality of services provided by CVs met the standards required for household progression toward resilience, stability, and eventual graduation from the programme.

I led the Case Managers and field teams who conducted direct household visits across all communities within the implementation sites, using structured verification checklists. An orientation was held for ad-hoc staff engaged to support the exercise, ensuring consistent application of the verification methodology across all sites. Community Volunteers were assessed on their responsiveness to household needs and their alignment with the overall goals of the HIFASS LOPIN-3 project. The exercise was carried out in close collaboration with the Senior Leadership Team, M&E Advisor, State Team Lead, Akwa Ibom M&E and Programme Units, CSO project teams, Case Managers, Community Volunteers, and ad-hoc support staff — ensuring comprehensive and coordinated coverage across all implementation communities.

Systematically, by verifying OVC service delivery at the household level on a monthly basis, there is strengthened data integrity, reinforced CV accountability, and ensured that enrolled households were genuinely receiving the services reported — directly safeguarding the quality and credibility of programme data submitted through NOMIS.

June 2019

Validation of OVC Households across communities - LOPIN 2 to LOPIN 3 Transitioning Exercise
Data Quality Auditing (DQA) - Akwa Ibom State

I led a comprehensive five-week Orphans and Vulnerable Children (OVC) Data Quality Auditing (DQA) exercise for the Transitioning of enrolled OVC Households in Akwa Ibom State from LOPIN 2 to LOPIN 3. As the State MEAL Officer, I coordinated a team of other MEAL and Research Assistants drawn from diverse cultural backgrounds across the State.

The exercise involved 7 Community Based Organisations (CBOs) implementing across over 18 LGAs in Akwa Ibom State. In line with the need to address the rising HIV prevalence in the state, following the National AIDS Indicator and Impact Survey (NAIIS), the scope of the project was expanded to include the Paediatric ART Saturation Strategy (PASS), ensuring that the project enrols all Children Living with HIV/AIDS (CLHIVs) in the State.

The auditing included the verification of the household addresses, and validation of the household folders for all active OVC clients within the Fiscal Year/Reporting period, ensuring data accuracy and integrity across implementing partners. Triangulation of primary (Household Folders) and Secondary (NOMIS) data sources were carried out by comparing generated and reviewed reports from the National OVC Management Information System (NOMIS). These records were cross-referenced against submitted reports by the implementing partners.

We also validated by service delivery by the available Health Centers, One-Stop Shops (OSS) and expert clients on the ground through phone calls to the identified Reactive Households.

Collaboration & Stakeholder Engagement ensured that the exercise was successful. We leveraged the community case workers, M&E and Data Entry Clerks of the Implementing Partner and Community-Based Organisation (CBO) who were responsible for folder retrieval and identification of household locations, ensuring an accountable process throughout.

April 2019

Open Defecation Free (ODF) Perception of Community members
Akamkpa, and Yakurr LGAs, Cross River State - Nigeria

I led an assessment focusing on assessing community perceptions, awareness, and behavioural attitudes towards the Open Defecation Free (ODF) campaign in Cross River State, particularly within communities across Yakurr and Akamkpa Local Government Area, with support from United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF). This survey formed part of broader Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) interventions aimed at eliminating open defecation and improving sanitation practices across communities.

The survey leveraged Focused Group Discussions (FGDs) with Men, Women, Young girls and Young boys across all the visited communities to have a holistic approach to their perceptions and the use of latrines.

The perception study examined residents’ knowledge of the health risks associated with open defecation, the availability and use of household latrines, and the level of community acceptance of improved sanitation behaviours. Findings revealed that awareness of open defecation is relatively high, the knowledge of its health implications were still limited and influenced by factors such as inadequate sanitation infrastructure, cultural practices, farming practices and the zeal to have a natural environment feel.

The assessment helped provide evidence for strengthening behaviour change communication, community mobilization, and policy implementation toward achieving sustainable sanitation. It also supported ongoing efforts by UNICEF and government partners to expand ODF status across the state. Notably, Yakurr has previously been declared an ODF local government area following extensive community-led sanitation (CLTS) interventions that promoted toilet construction and hygiene education.

August - September 2018

Community-Based Operations Research on HIV Risk, Testing & Treatment Retention
Akamkpa, Calabar Municipal and Calabar South LGAs, Cross River State - Nigeria

As M&E Officer for Cross River State, and part of the HIFASS LOPIN-3 MEAL team, I supported the design and led a community-based operations research study conducted from 20 August to 10 September 2018 across Scale-up LGAs (Calabar Municipal and Calabar South) and a Sustained Support LGA (Akamkpa) in Cross River State. This involved five CSO partners: Oten Ita Foundation (OTF), Positive Development Foundation (PDF), Neighbourhood Care-Well Foundation (NCF), Rhema Care (RC), and Calabar Archdiocesan Committee on AIDS (CAACA)

Research Context & Rationale: Programme data had revealed critical gaps in the HIV continuum of care among enrolled OVC households: not all beneficiaries knew their HIV status, not all HIV-positive individuals were on treatment, and the majority of those in treatment were not adhering to their treatment regimen. In pursuit of the global 90-90-90 targets, this cross-sectional study was designed to uncover the underlying social and cultural factors driving these gaps — comparing dynamics across both scale-up and sustained support sites to inform targeted programmatic responses.

Research Objectives: The study aimed to identify social and cultural factors predisposing enrolled OVCs (aged 0–17 years) to new HIV infections; determine barriers to HIV testing and status awareness; identify factors that promoted or hindered ART acceptance and retention in treatment; and understand the drivers of non-adherence to treatment regimens among enrolled households.

Methodology & Team: The study deployed 10 trained data collectors supervised by 4 case monitors across enrolled households in the target LGAs over seven days of field work. Five data entry clerks subsequently captured completed questionnaires into a purpose-built CSPro database over five days for analysis. Local guides supported community navigation and access, while the full M&E team provided technical oversight throughout.

Sampling & Design: Using the NOMIS household enrolment register, 320 households were selected through stratified sampling — 70 of which were identified as HIV-positive households with reactive caregivers and/or children. In a key design safeguard, the HIV status of selected households was deliberately withheld from Research Assistants during data collection to eliminate bias in the interview process.

Data Collection: Ten Research Assistants and CSO M&E Officers received a two-day orientation on the structured questionnaire before being deployed to the field. Each Research Assistant was allocated 31–41 households to visit within a seven-day data collection window, supported by community volunteers as local guides for household access. At the close of fieldwork, 318 of the 320 targeted questionnaires were successfully completed — a 99.4% completion rate.

Data Entry & Analysis: Completed questionnaires were entered into a CSPro database by a dedicated data entry team over two days. Data analysis was subsequently conducted by the M&E team using SPSS and EPIData to identify trends and assess improvements in project implementation. Preliminary findings were presented at the FY18 Quarter 4 Performance Review Meeting, with a full draft report submitted to the Director of M&E and Senior Leadership Team for review and onward dissemination to stakeholders.

Ethical Compliance: The study followed a formal ethical clearance process, ensuring that all research activities were conducted in accordance with national and institutional standards for human subjects research.

Strategic Significance: By systematically investigating socio-cultural determinants of HIV risk and treatment behaviour at community level, this research provided HIFASS LOPIN-3 with an evidence base to redesign programme strategies; directly supporting improved HIV status awareness, linkage to treatment, and viral load suppression among vulnerable children and households in Cross River State.

August to September 2018

Mapping of Reactive OVC Households using Geographic Information System (GIS)
All Project Implementation Communities and LGAs, Cross River State.

As part of the HIFASS LOPIN-3 project, I supported a GIS mapping exercise conducted from 28 August to 1 September 2018 across 14 project implementation LGAs in Cross River State, targeting reactive households — HIV-positive OVC clients and caregivers enrolled in the programme.

Methodology: Using mobile Android devices loaded with the KoboCollect application, the team conducted house-to-house verification visits to reactive households as listed by CSO partners. GPS coordinates were captured for each verified household and for the health facilities where beneficiaries accessed HIV treatment, creating a spatial dataset linking clients to their care points. A total of 78 households were visited across both LGAs.

Key Findings & Follow-Up: Field visits revealed that several reactive households were not adhering to their treatment regimens, with some clients stockpiling medication without returning for refills. On-site adherence counselling was immediately provided, with further follow-up assigned to CSO partners. A notable lesson emerged from the exercise: some reactive households were travelling as far as 30 km to access treatment, highlighting a significant access barrier requiring further investigation and targeted response to connect clients to closer facilities.

September 2018

FY18 Quarter 4 Project Performance Review Meeting — HIFASS LOPIN-3
Calabar, Cross River State

As M&E Officer and co-Activity Manager alongside the Director of M&E, I co-facilitated the FY18 Quarter 4 Performance Review Meeting held on 14–15 September 2018 at Naks Hotel, Calabar — a critical two-day exercise bringing together CSO Programme Managers, M&E Officers, and data clerks in preparation for the FY18 Annual Progress Report (APR18).

The meeting was structured around four key objectives: assessing where the project stood against targets, triangulating data across LOPIN-3, CSOs, and government partners, identifying performance gaps across all PEPFAR indicators, and agreeing on remediation strategies before the close of the quarter. Each CSO presented its Quarter 4 performance data (July–August 2018) across the full range of OVC indicators — including OVC_SERV, OVC_HIVSTAT, OVC_RISKASS, OVC_HTSLINK, OVC_ARTSUPP, OVC_BIRTHCERT, OVC_HIVPREV, OVC_EDU, OVC_NUTRITION, and OVC_TXLINK — after which technical staff provided structured feedback and gap remediation plans were documented.

Key Gaps Identified & Actions Agreed: The review surfaced significant gaps across multiple CSOs: RHEMA CARE had 2,528 OVCs yet to be served in September, with many PLHIV enrolled through support groups untraceable due to inaccurate contact details; IPGH had 179 OVCs with unknown HIV status requiring urgent risk assessment; OTEN had 981 OVCs left to be served and 2,405 pending HIV risk assessments, alongside discrepancies between NOMIS and monthly summary forms; and PDF had enrolled 4,413 beneficiaries against a target of 3,139 while operating in overlapping wards with NCF. Clear action plans and timelines were agreed for each CSO to close their gaps before APR reporting.

Data Quality & Programmatic Guidance: A significant cross-cutting finding was widespread data inconsistency between CSO monthly summary forms and NOMIS — CSOs were formally directed that only NOMIS data would count for APR reporting, reinforcing the primacy of the system. Additional programmatic guidance was issued on referral completion standards, Child Protection indicator reporting using the CPN tool, and obtaining death certificates from NPoPC for deceased enrolled PLHIV. Preliminary findings from the ongoing operational research were also presented, with early results indicating positive project impact across implementation communities
