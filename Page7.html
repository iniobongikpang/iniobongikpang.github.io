July 2018

Supportive Supervision and Validation of Transitioned Households — LOPIN 3 Project
FHI 360/HIFASS, Calabar Municipal and Calabar South LGAs, Cross River State

As part of ongoing M&E strengthening under the HIFASS LOPIN-3 project, I conducted a supportive supervision visit to two CSO partners — Kejie Health Foundation in Ogoja and Oten Ita Foundation in Obudu — on 27 July 2018, providing hands-on technical assistance across three core areas: NOMIS troubleshooting and data clean-up, Monthly Summary Form (MSF) review, and documentation audit of graduated and active households.

NOMIS Troubleshooting & Data Clean-Up: At Kejie Health Foundation, 71 flagged duplicate household names were investigated — 68 were confirmed as coincidental matches across different wards, caregivers, and OVCs, while 3 genuine duplicates were merged. At Oten Ita Foundation, 62 flagged duplicates were reviewed, with 5 confirmed factual duplicates merged. A ward correction was also processed for one household erroneously assigned to Buda when the actual residence was Boje. Additional technical assistance was provided to resolve system-level NOMIS challenges, including households stuck on withdrawal lists, unenrolled graduation statuses, and unupdated enrolment records — addressed through ad-hoc database operations.

MSF Review: Technical assistance was provided to both CSOs in reviewing their June Monthly Summary Forms prior to submission, with data discrepancies identified and immediately corrected by M&E Officers at both sites.

Documentation Audit — Graduated & Active Households: Random samples of 20 case folders were reviewed at each CSO. At Kejie, key gaps included missing CPA and case closure signatures, undocumented CPA scores, absence of medication adherence monitoring checklists for HIV-positive VCs and caregivers, and 42% of graduated households not reflected in NOMIS. At Oten Ita, similar gaps were identified — including undocumented caregiver services, missing case closure records for 10% of graduated households, and 10% of graduations not updated in NOMIS. Positive findings at both sites included complete birth certificate documentation for all graduated households and well-documented VC service forms.

Recommendations & Follow-Up: Specific, time-bound corrective actions were issued to both CSOs by 25 July 2018, covering graduation folder updates in NOMIS, caregiver service data entry, deployment of medication adherence monitoring tools, and completion of all required CPA and case closure documentation — with follow-up assigned to respective M&E Officers and data clerks.

LOPIN 3 Project received enrolled households as transitioned from FHI 360, as an implementing partner in Cross River State. I led the project team for the validation of transitioned households through visitation following enrolled addressed and handing over. Capacity building for the CBOs was also held for the transitioning of the NOMIS database.

June 2018

Quarterly Field-Based Data Quality Assessment (DQA) — Cross River States
Implementation LGAs, Cross River State

As M&E Officer for Cross River State on the HIFASS LOPIN-3 project, I designed and led a quarterly field-based Data Quality Assessment (DQA) conducted from 5–9 June 2018 across CSO partner offices and sites in Cross River and Ebonyi States, covering 3 CSO partners, 15 M&E Officers and data clerks, and 23 LGAs.

Purpose & Scope:The DQA was conducted to ensure the validity and reliability of OVC service data reported across all LOPIN-3 CSO partners in both states. All CSOs were included in the scope — a deliberate decision taken ahead of an anticipated USAID DQA to ensure that every partner's data was prepared and verifiable. The assessment applied four standardised quality checks to OVC service data: completeness, timeliness, consistency, and reliability. Data quality scores were calculated objectively to rank each CSO's performance, with a zero tolerance policy enforced on fraudulent or fictitious data.

Methodology: The DQA was conducted directly at CSO offices and implementation sites, enabling the M&E team to review source documents, cross-check reported data against primary records, and provide real-time corrective guidance to M&E Officers and data clerks. CSO M&E units had been previously trained on DQA methodology, and this field exercise applied and reinforced those skills in practice.

Coordination: The exercise was carried out by a multi-state M&E team comprising the Director of M&E (DME), M&E Advisor, M&E Officers for Cross River and Ebonyi States, M&E Assistants, and ad-hoc OVCPA staff — ensuring consistent and thorough coverage across all 23 LGAs and three CSO partners.

Overall, by institutionalising quarterly field-based DQAs with objective scoring and zero tolerance for data fabrication, we strengthened the integrity of OVC programme data across two states; directly supporting accountability to USAID and ensuring that reported service delivery figures accurately reflected conditions on the ground.

Federal and State MDAs in Cross River State.

As the Program/M&E Officer and Manager, I served as the representative of the Civil Society Organisation (CSOs) between 2013 and 2018. Being trained in observation of Public Procurement processes by the Bureau of Public Procurement (BPP), I joined in observing tender procurement processes. Universal Basic Education (UBE) projects at the State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB) in Cross River State in March 2013 and July 2014.

The monitoring covered tender opening and prequalification exercises for all level of works inclduing infrastructure/construction and supply projects supporting Special Education Centres across all the LGAs of the State including classroom construction, rehabilitation works, educational aids, furniture, and supporting facilities.

During the observation, multiple bids were opened and reviewed in the presence of government officials, contractors, and oversight bodies, including the Due Process and Price Intelligence Bureau (DPPIB) and the Bureau of Public Procurement (BPP).

The related MDAs where observations were carried out included:

  • Cross River Basin Development Authority (CRBDA)
  • Ministry of Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

  • State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB)
  • Cross River State Agricultural Development Programme (CRADP)
  • Border Service Commission (BORDERCOM)
  • Cross River State Minisstry of International Donor Corporation

Overall, the monitoring reinforced BTAN’s role in promoting transparency, improving procurement standards, and strengthening public expenditure oversight within the education sector.

January 2013 to February 2018

Partners' Coordination & Review Meetings
Cross River State Donor-Assisted Projects.

As a civil society accountability partner within Cross River State's multi-stakeholder donor coordination architecture, BTAN participated in the monthly Partners' Coordination and Review Meetings convened by the Cross River State Department of International Development Cooperation (DIDC). These forums brought together representatives from over 20 donor-assisted programmes and government agencies including IFAD, UNICEF, EU, World Bank-supported programmes, FADAMA, YESSO, CR-RAMP, RUWATSSA, UNFPA, NEWMAP, SACA, and multiple state ministries with BTAN as the sole civil society voice in this multi-partner governance space.

Cross-Sectoral Programme Oversight: Across all meetings, I tracked progress and implementation gaps across a broad range of sectors. In agriculture and livelihoods, FADAMA III was advancing preparations for additional World Bank financing, while IFAD reported the formation of Community Apex Development Associations, completion of processing mills, and a shift from subsistence to commercial farming — though non-payment of state counterpart funds remained a persistent challenge across multiple programmes. In water and sanitation, EU-WSSSRP Phase II commenced in 2013 and had progressed to baseline surveys and infrastructure design by 2014, with coordination concerns raised around overlap with the Tulsi Chanrai Foundation in Yakurr and Boki LGAs. On social protection, YESSO — with Cross River State being the first state in Nigeria to implement Community-Based Targeting for the Unified Registry of Beneficiaries — was advancing toward full implementation across 12 LGAs. On infrastructure, road construction under NPFS, CR-RAMP, and CADP was progressing, opening access to remote communities in Akamkpa, Obanliku, and Obudu LGAs.

Accountability & Governance Contributions: A recurring theme across both meetings was the need to strengthen coordination, reporting standards, and financial accountability among donor-assisted programmes. Key governance developments included the establishment of a centralised project tracking database at DIDC; conditionality of counterpart fund releases on timely reporting; a formal attendance and accountability policy under which partners absent for three consecutive meetings risked sanctions; and the adoption of a rotating meeting hosting schedule across 21 organisations, institutionalising the coordination forum. A Sector-Wide Approach (SWAp) in agriculture was also announced, and a state-wide MDG scorecard was initiated requiring all partners to supply project completion data through their parent MDAs.

Strategic Value: BTAN's consistent presence in this state-level forum — as a recognised accountability actor rather than merely a project implementer — enabled tracking of donor-funded commitments, early identification of cross-sectoral implementation gaps, and monitoring of public and donor resource management across health, infrastructure, water, agriculture, and social protection. The intelligence gathered directly informed BTAN's budget advocacy, expenditure monitoring activities, and stakeholder engagement across Cross River State.

January 2013 to December 2017

Review of the Cross River State Appropriation Bill - Annual Budgets
Production of the 2013 Citizen's Budget - A simplified version of the Budget.

I led the production of the Citizens' Guide to the Cross River State 2013 Budget; a plain-language simplification of the official state budget designed to make public finance accessible to ordinary citizens. Tagged the "Budget of Delivery" the 2013 budget totalled ₦152.4 billion and was framed around the administration's core goal of wealth creation through economic development. BTAN's involvement in producing and disseminating this guide reflects its broader mandate to promote budget transparency and citizen participation in public finance management.

Purpose of the Citizens' Guide: The guide was created explicitly to demystify the state budget, translating a complex government financial document into accessible information that any citizen could understand. This aligns directly with BTAN's advocacy for open, accountable governance and its work in supporting civil society engagement with state budget processes.

Government's Seven-Point Development Agenda: The 2013 budget was anchored on Cross River State's Seven-Point Development Agenda, spanning agriculture, tourism, education, primary healthcare, private sector development, infrastructure, and environmental conservation. Underpinning these priorities were five key reform programmes: public service reform, local government reform, social welfare reform, tax system reform, and public procurement reform — the last of which directly intersects with BTAN's procurement transparency work.

Key Sectoral Allocations: The budget's headline allocations across priority sectors were as follows. Education received the largest share at ₦22.4 billion, funding UBE counterpart contributions and the establishment of a new State Polytechnic in Ugep. Health was allocated ₦7.8 billion, sustaining Project HOPE — which provides free healthcare to pregnant women and children under five — alongside HIV/AIDS interventions and MDG-related programmes. Water Resources received ₦6.5 billion for rural, semi-urban, and urban water infrastructure, sanitation, and hygiene. Social Development, Youth and Sport was allocated ₦3.0 billion, supporting capacity building and preparations to host the 2014 National Sports Festival.

These were done for every other year's appropriation bill:

  • 2014: ‘Budget of Progression’
  • 2015: ‘Budget of Transition’
  • 2016: ‘Budget of Deep Vision’
  • 2017: ‘Budget of Infinite Transposition’

Strategic Significance: The production and dissemination of this Citizens' Budget Guide represents one of BTAN's core outputs in its budget transparency work. By translating technical appropriations into citizen-friendly language and distributing this knowledge at the grassroots level, BTAN directly supported public oversight of government spending — empowering communities to track whether budgeted allocations in health, education, and infrastructure were actually delivered. This work complements BTAN's procurement monitoring and health sector advocacy documented across other portfolio pieces.

December 2017

Capacity Building - Advanced Family Planning (AFP) Advocacy SMART Tool
Cross River State Family Planning Advocacy Working Group (FPAWG).

As a member of the Cross River State Family Planning Advocacy Working Group (FPAWG) and as part of the efforts to strengthen my engagement with the Cross River State House of Assembly in ensuring improved access to Family Planning Commodities, I participated the capacity Building session on Advanced Family Planning (AFP) facilitated by Pathfinder International, and funded by USAID.

The AFP SMART advocacy tool is an evidence-based approach used by Pathfinder International to strengthen advocacy for family planning policies and programs. Grounded in the SMART principle (Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, and Time-bound), the tool provides civil society organizations, youth groups, and health advocates with a structured framework to influence government decisions on reproductive health and family planning. It emphasizes clear objectives, data-driven messaging, and targeted engagement with key decision-makers.

Through the AFP SMART approach, advocates identify specific “quick wins” that can be achieved in the short term while contributing to broader family planning goals. The tool guides users through structured planning, from problem identification to achieving measurable advocacy outcomes, using research, community evidence, and strategic engagement with policymakers. This approach ensures that advocacy efforts are focused, effective, and accountable, increasing the likelihood of policy and funding wins.

In Cross River State, and Nigeria, Pathfinder International leveraged the AFP SMART tool to train advocacy groups, youth networks, and civil society organizations. These efforts have supported government commitment tracking, advocacy for dedicated budget lines for adolescent and youth sexual and reproductive health, and improved access to voluntary family planning services. The tool has contributed to measurable advocacy successes, including policy improvements and increased resources for family planning programs at state and national levels.

January 2014 to December 2017

Engagement with the Cross River State House of Assembly Members
Review of the Cross River State Appropriation Bill - Annual Budgets.

Yearly, BTAN makes a formal presentation to the Cross River State House of Assembly (CRSHA) Committee on Finance and Appropriation. At the 2014 Budget Stakeholders' Forum, the presentation on the review of the Appropriation Bill was made. The 2014 budget, tagged the "Budget of Progression" totalled ₦176.3 billion and was framed as the state's MDG completion budget, with Cross River State setting 2014 as its internal target date for achieving the Millennium Development Goals. BTAN used this platform to conduct a detailed, line-by-line scrutiny of the draft budget across multiple ministries, departments and agencies (MDAs), flagging irregularities and making evidence-based recommendations to the legislature.

BTAN undertook a comprehensive review of the budget document, analysing allocations and budget lines across more than 15 MDAs. The review identified four recurring categories of concern: duplicated budget lines (where the same project appeared under multiple MDAs or multiple times within the same MDA), vague or ambiguous budget lines lacking project specifications or locations, misclassified expenditure items, and potentially unnecessary or politically motivated projects. These were compiled into a structured query-and-advice table presented directly to the House Committee.

Key findings by sectors were:

  • On health, BTAN found the health budget at 5.32% of total expenditure — well below the 15% Abuja Declaration benchmark — and raised this as a serious concern given the state's ongoing challenges with maternal and child health and HIV/AIDS. A critical gap identified was a nil allocation for Family Planning Commodities (FPC) distribution, despite the Federal Government having already procured hundreds of millions of naira worth of contraceptives for the state. BTAN noted that without a state budget line for distribution, these commodities were failing to reach the 371 service delivery points across Cross River State, and recommended an allocation of ₦10 million to cover this gap — directly connecting to BTAN's RH-PAIN advocacy work.
  • On education, BTAN commended the sector's allocation of ₦23.7 billion (approximately 13.46% of the budget), noting it was at least half of UNESCO's 26% benchmark, and welcomed the ₦55 million grant-in-aid to 11 missionary schools. However, it flagged the need for vocational education investment to reduce dependence on external capacity for middle and low-level manpower.
  • On agriculture, BTAN advocated for revitalisation of irrigation schemes in the northern and central senatorial districts, and urged the state to make counterpart fund provision for FADAMA III+ Additional Financing, warning that failure to do so would forfeit World Bank funding.
  • On tourism, BTAN flagged a serious fiscal imbalance — proposed tourism-related expenditure of approximately ₦2.74 billion against projected inflows of only ₦243.8 million — raising concerns about the sustainability of the sector's spending plans.
  • On procurement and governance, BTAN identified consultancy spending of approximately ₦2 billion across MDAs, questioned the value for money given six years of continuous consultant engagement without apparent capacity transfer, and recommended competitive engagement of CSOs as a cost-effective alternative. BTAN also raised concerns about a budget line proposing government formation of a CSO/NGO advocacy network, warning this would compromise civil society independence and accountability.

Duplication & Fiscal Leakage Analysis: BTAN's line-by-line review identified potential budget leakages of approximately ₦3 billion arising from duplicated or unnecessary projects across MDAs including the Ministries of Works, Environment, Education, Women Affairs, Social Welfare, and the House of Assembly itself. Specific duplications flagged included traffic light projects appearing in both Ministry of Works and DOPT, street drainage appearing in multiple MDAs, and identical road projects listed under different budget lines.

BTAN also specifically recommended that the House of Assembly should budget for its own legislative budget office, noting that while the office existed, it had no budget line for research or technical support, undermining the legislature's capacity to exercise meaningful oversight.

Recommendations: BTAN's formal recommendations included: adherence to the Medium-Term Expenditure Framework (MTEF) to reduce leakages; a moratorium on brand new projects in 2014 in favour of completing existing projects; elimination of identified ₦3 billion in duplications; clearer and more specific budget lines across all MDAs; reduction in the proliferation of new MDAs to reduce governance costs; and explicit linkage between budget consultative forum resolutions and final appropriations.

These were done for every other year's appropriation bill:

  • 2015: ‘Budget of Transition’
  • 2016: ‘Budget of Deep Vision’
  • 2017: ‘Budget of Infinite Transposition’

Strategic Significance: This presentation is one of BTAN's most comprehensive outputs, demonstrating its capacity to engage the legislature as a technical accountability partner, not merely a civil society observer. By presenting a structured, evidence-based critique of a ₦176 billion budget directly to the House Committee on Finance and Appropriation, BTAN performed a legislative support function that directly strengthened the state's budget oversight architecture. The opportunity was leveraged to again, continue BTAN's advocacy, integrating her thematic work which was connecting its RH-PAIN family planning advocacy, its procurement transparency monitoring, and its donor coordination engagement into a single, unified budget accountability submission.

May 2016

End Violence Against Children (EndVAC) Campaign
Calabar - Cross River State, Nigeria

As the Program/M&E Manager, I led the implementation of the iMarch4Children – End Violence Against Children (EndVAC) campaign in Cross River State, organized by the Budget Transparency and Accountability Initiative, Nigeria (BTAN) in partnership with Playback Nigeria Initiative and UNICEF. The campaign was part of a nationwide advocacy effort to raise awareness about the prevalence of violence against children and to promote stronger child protection systems.

The initiative mobilized students, civil society organisations, community stakeholders, and government institutions for an art-based advocacy march in Calabar to amplify calls for the protection of children’s rights. Engagements were conducted with key stakeholders including the Nigerian Police Force, Federal Road Safety Corps, Ministry of Women Affairs, media organizations, and secondary schools to ensure broad participation and visibility.

The campaign culminated in a march and presentation of a communiqué to the Cross River State House of Assembly, advocating for the prosecution of offenders, effective implementation of the Child Rights Act, improved psychosocial support for survivors, and stronger reporting and response mechanisms for cases of violence against children.

The initiative strengthened public awareness, multi-stakeholder collaboration, and legislative engagement toward advancing child protection and promoting a culture of zero tolerance for violence against children.
