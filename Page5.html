May 2021

Rapid Needs Assessment for New Influx of Cameroonian Refugees
Shibong Community - Takum LGA, Taraba State - Nigeria

Parts of southern Cameroon randomly continue to experience crises and this leads to continuous arrival of Cameroon Nationals into the border communities of Nigeria seeking asylum. As the protection partner for the UNHCR project in Taraba State, JRS consistently monitors these border communities to ensure that newly arrived Cameroonians received an all-inclusive protection. The protection team from JRS UNHCR and BPRM projects conducted a needs assessment for newly arrived Cameroonians (influx) in Shibong community in Takum LGA on 19th of May 2021. A few villages (Dong camp 2 and Obum) within Shibong community were visited.

I led the Project team (Protection, MHPSS, Legal, Education Units) and we met with the village head and the asylum seekers who newly arrived. The asylum seekers expressed their joy and appreciation for the teams’ visit while sharing their challenges and scrambling for safety with the renewed crises in Cameroon. However, they stated that most of the older persons could not run while the pregnant women in the cause of flight went into early labour.

The interactions gave an insight on their basic needs, which included:

  • Need for shelter.
  • Lack of Food.
  • Lack of access to Water and Sanitation Hygiene.
  • Need for mosquito nets.
  • Need for access to land for farming (livelihood support).
  • Need to access health services (no health facility within the villages).
  • Need to access educational support.

Further discussions revealed that many other Cameroonians who fled because of the renewed crises settled in some other border communities. They stated that these communities (Manga, Omaojowe, Onunkun, Oyanlekin, Kongkem and Omankwun {Fulani dominated}) are hard-to-reach areas with difficult terrains that are not motorable.

The team, with support from the Community leaders leveraged on the opportunity to create awareness on Conflict Related Sexual Violence (CRSV), Gender Based Violence (GBV), and their related effects and consequences. They were encouraged to take advantage of the secure reporting channel made available through fliers, should any CRSV or GBV case arise.

The Community leaders committed to ensuring that available resources within the communities would be shared with the asylum seekers while also advocating for timely support to the newly arrived Cameroonians from JRS and other UN partner organizations.

March - April 2021

Profiling and Needs Assessment of Cameroonian Refugees – Taraba State
Persons of Concern (PoCs) under the age of 18 years.

A comprehensive profiling and needs assessment was conducted to better understand the situation of Persons of Concern (PoCs) under 18 years in Taraba State. The exercise focused on identifying vulnerabilities, protection risks, and service gaps affecting refugee children and adolescents within host communities.

The assessment captured detailed data on education access, child protection concerns, health needs, psychosocial wellbeing, family structures, and living conditions. Particular attention was given to out-of-school children, unaccompanied and separated children, children in foster care, and those with special needs.

Targeted categories:
  • Out-of-School Children (OOSC): Children and adolescents not currently enrolled in formal or non-formal education.
  • Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC): Children living without their parents or primary caregivers.
  • Children in Foster Care: Minors placed with extended family members or host households due to displacement.
  • Children with Special Needs: Including children living with disabilities or chronic health conditions requiring additional support.
  • Adolescent Girls at Risk: Particularly those vulnerable to early marriage, exploitation, or gender-based violence.
  • Youth Under 25 with Interrupted Education: Young refugees previously enrolled in tertiary institutions whose studies were disrupted by displacement.

Findings from the assessment are guiding targeted programming, strengthening case management, and informing advocacy efforts to improve access to education, protection services, and essential support systems. The results also provide evidence for designing child-sensitive interventions that promote safety, inclusion, and long-term resilience for refugee and host community children.

Key Findings

The assessment revealed significant vulnerabilities among refugee children and adolescents in Taraba State, highlighting urgent protection and service gaps.

Protection Risks:Unaccompanied and separated children (UASCs), as well as children in foster care, faced heightened protection risks, including neglect, exploitation, and inadequate supervision. Many households lacked adequate documentation and child protection awareness.

Education:A high number of children were out of school due to financial constraints, lack of learning materials, language barriers, and limited access to nearby schools. Some adolescents had interrupted secondary or tertiary education as a result of displacement.

Economic Vulnerability:Most households reported low and unstable income sources, limiting their ability to meet children’s basic needs such as food, healthcare, clothing, and school-related expenses.

Psychosocial Wellbeing:Children exhibited signs of emotional distress linked to exposure to violence, displacement, and family separation. Access to structured psychosocial support services was limited.

Health and Basic Needs:Access to healthcare services, hygiene materials, and adequate shelter remained a challenge for several families, particularly in hard-to-reach communities.

Overall, the findings underscore the need for integrated, child-focused interventions that combine education support, protection services, livelihood strengthening, and psychosocial care to promote safety, inclusion, and resilience.

March 2021

Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) for Newly arrived Cameroonian Refugees
Takum, Ussa and Sardauna LGAs – Taraba State.

As an outcome of the influx of refugees within the period, the protection needs of the new arrivals as well as the host communities called for more attention. In response to these, I supported Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) channelled to assess the Protection and Food Security Needs of the refugees within the host communities held in Takum and Ussa LGAs on 29th March while in Sarduana LGA, it held on 31st March 2021. These FGDs targeted four groups (Men, Women, Boys and Girls) across all locations.

Targeted groups were:
  • In Takum town, Takum LGA the FGD was held with 3 refugee groups (Women, Girls and Men).
  • In Lissam community, Ussa LGA the FGD was held with the 2 refugee groups (Women/men and Girls) and 1 host community group (men).
  • In Gembu and Nguroje communities, Sardauna LGA the FGD.

From the refugee population, their shared challenges included:

  • WASH Needs: Insufficient supply of potable water to the refugee population especially in Ussa LGA. This has brought about a high cost of accessing water from the few available water vendors within the community as they have to pay as high as N500.00 to get water.
  • Education Needs: Most of the young girls agreed that they would love to be enrolled into post-secondary educational system to enable them further their educational pursuit within the State.
  • Shelter Needs:For those who had rented houses expresses that the property owners have not been patient and considerate in cases where they do not have immediate cash to renew their rents on expiration.
  • Health Needs: Based on protections services available, they agreed that access to health services has improved relatively than it was in 2020.
  • Security Needs:The freedom of movement for the refugees is also a concern as the community tax force harasses the refugees especially those who carry foodstuff either for personal or business gains. Harassment of refugees especially those who are commercial motorcycle riders as the community members who get transported from one location to another refuses to pay for the services.

The Host community population shared their challenges included:

  • WASH Needs: They agreed that supply of potable water to the community dwellers Lissam, Ussa LGA had become challenging due to the increased population as a result of the refugees who are now resident among them. This has brought about a high cost in managing and ensuring that water is made available and accessible in the community.
  • Cost of living: They agreed that the cost of food materials were generally high in Ussa LGA due to the high cost of transporting food materials to the community from the border town.

  • Jesuit Refugee Service (JRS) – Protection Partner

January - February 2021

Distribution of Food and Non-Food Items to Cameroonian Refugees and Host Community Members
Takum, Ussa, Kurmi & Sardauna LGAs, Taraba State - Nigeria

The Protection for Cameroonian Refugees in Taraba State project was aimed at improving the protection and access to quality education of the refugees residing in host communities within Taraba State. Most of these refugees lived in remote and inaccessible areas and in most cases, near the international borders with Cameroon. Due to the displacements, these fleeing households lack access to basic needs and food for their daily survival.

I led the distribution of Non-Food Items to PoCs in Gembu and Nguroje in Sardauna LGA in January 2021. This was a continuation of the exercise which began in 2020 but was not concluded because of the festive season. A total of 1,075 households with 3,433 individuals benefitted from this distribution - (917 households with 2,952 individuals in Gembu and 158 households with 481 individuals in Nguroje).

NFIs distributed included customized mosquito nets, blankets, kitchen sets, mats, buckets and jerrycans.

Furthermore, I led the distribution of food items, giving priority to outstanding Households from the previous distribution across the four (4) project LGAs hosting refugees. Beneficiaries of the food distribution were also considered based on agreed vulnerability criteria including households with aged parents, people living with disabilities and people who are chronically ill.

The food items distributed included rice, garri, palm oil, salt, and wheat flour reaching a total of 1,503 individuals in 619 households.

January to March 2020

Research Manager: Community-Based Health Workers & HIV/AIDS Epidemic Control — Nigeria
Selected LGAs in Akwa Ibom and Cross River States.

I led a team of Research Assistants on a peer-reviewed cross-sectional study investigating the roles of informal Community-Based Health Workers (CBHWs) in Nigeria's pursuit of the global UNAIDS 95-95-95 HIV/AIDS epidemic control targets by 2030. My contribution included training of Research Assistants, Data collection and data entry, cleaning and analysis across the study sites in Akwa Ibom and Cross River States.

Background: Various categories of informal community-based health workers (CBHWs) across sub-Saharan Africa carry out different functions to address HIV/AIDS including support of the patients and families and support of the health system. A cross-sectional survey was conducted to investigate and document the characteristics and the specific roles of informal CBHWs in Nigeria's pursuit of the HIV/AIDS epidemic control targets of 95-95-95 by 2030.

Methodology: Quantitative data were collected using a semi-structured questionnaire to elicit responses from the community-based health workers. Of CBHWs, 261 were Community Volunteers (CVs), 83 were Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs), 297 were Patent Medicine Sellers (PMVs), and 3 were Traditional Healers(THLs).

Study Overview: The study surveyed 642 informal CBHWs across four Local Government Areas in Akwa Ibom and Cross River States, spanning four categories: Community Volunteers (CVs), Patent Medicine Vendors (PMVs), Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs), and Traditional Healers. The research aimed to document the characteristics, specific roles, and operational challenges of these workers in relation to Nigeria's HIV/AIDS response.

Key Findings: The majority of CBHWs were female (62%), aged 25–49, with secondary or higher education. While 63% of respondents contributed to the first 95 (HIV testing and status awareness) and 55% to the second 95 (linkage and retention in treatment), only 38% were engaged in activities supporting the third 95 — viral load monitoring and suppression. Community Volunteers demonstrated the highest levels of engagement across all three targets, while PMVs and TBAs showed significantly lower participation, particularly in treatment adherence and viral load tracking.

Key Conclusions: The study found that informal CBHWs are substantially underutilised relative to their potential, with Nigeria's existing task-shifting policy guidelines limiting the scope of their roles. It recommended a review of these guidelines to broaden CBHW responsibilities — particularly in ARV adherence monitoring and viral load facilitation — and called for stronger government and community involvement in the recruitment, training, and sustaining of this critical health workforce.

January 2020

Project Close-Out Ceremony - Funded by USAID
Local OVC Partners in Nigeria - Region 3 (LOPIN-3)/Pediatric ART Saturation Strategy (PASS)

The HIFASS LOPIN 3 Project, funded by USAID, successfully supported vulnerable children and households affected by HIV/AIDS across five states in Nigeria. Over five years, the project reached more than 184,000 children and 48,000 caregivers with integrated services including healthcare referrals, psychosocial support, education assistance, economic strengthening, and nutrition counseling.

Serving as the MEAL Officer on the project, I participated in the close-out ceremony of the project after 5 years of support OVC and CLHIV across Cross River and Akwa Ibom States.

Implemented by Health Initiatives for Safety and Stability in Africa, the project strengthened community systems by building the capacity of 26 local civil society organizations and supporting key government institutions to improve service delivery and responsiveness to vulnerable populations.

Key achievements include enhanced access to quality health and protection services, improved household resilience through livelihood support and job skills training, and increased school enrollment through targeted educational assistance. The project also contributed to reducing stigma associated with HIV and promoting inclusive community participation.

Overall, LOPIN 3 demonstrated a sustainable, community-driven model for supporting orphans and vulnerable children, with scalable approaches for future programming and government adoption.

September 2019

Annual MEAL Project Performance Review Meeting - USAID Funded LOPIN 3 Project
Assessment of CSO Performance - Akwa Ibom State

As the MEAL Officer for Akwa Ibom State on the HIFASS LOPIN-3 project, I designed and facilitated a two-day Quarterly Performance Review Meeting held in Uyo, Akwa Ibom State on 12–13 September 2019, bringing together CSO partners, government OVC desk officers, and the HIFASS M&E and programme teams for a structured performance review ahead of the FY19 Annual Progress Report (APR).

The meeting was designed to assess CSO implementation performance over the preceding two months (July and August 2019) against PEPFAR MER and Custom Indicators due for end-of-quarter reporting. Using data extracted and analysed from NOMIS, the review identified performance gaps across indicators, assessed programmatic progress, and developed targeted re-strategization plans to close those gaps within the final month of the quarter and ensuring CSOs could meet their reporting targets for the FY19 APR.

The meeting brought together a broad range of participants including the Senior Leadership Team, M&E Advisor, State Team Lead, the Akwa Ibom M&E and Programme Units, CSO programme and M&E Officers, data clerks, Case Managers, and Government of Nigeria OVC Desk Officers at both state (SACA) and LGA levels; reinforcing government ownership and coordination across the OVC programme.

By institutionalising the quarterly performance review with gap analysis and corrective strategy sessions, this activity strengthened the culture of data-driven decision-making among CSO partners and directly contributed to improved indicator performance and accountability across HIFASS LOPIN-3 implementation sites in Akwa Ibom State.
