July 2025

Project Inception Meeting and Stakeholder Engagement - The Basque Government, Phase II
Adamawa and Borno States, Northeast Nigeria

I lead the Program Team of Jesuit Refugee Service (JRS) Nigeria Country Office to Yola, Adamawa State from 15–18 July 2025 to formally launch the "Building Inclusive and Sustainable Leadership in Humanitarian Contexts: Gender-Sensitive Local Capacity Building in Borno and Adamawa"project, funded by the Basque Government.

the inception meeting brought together representatives from the Ministry of Agriculture, Ministry of Education, Ministry of Women Affairs, ADAS-P, the Education in Emergencies Working Group (EiEWG), community leaders, and agricultural extension agents. The session presented the project's objectives, expected outcomes, and implementation strategy, while gathering stakeholder insights to align the intervention with community needs and government priorities. Participants commended the project's inclusive and participatory approach and pledged support for inter-agency coordination, gender equity, and community ownership throughout implementation.

JRS Nigeria project staff participated in a comprehensive training covering gender and social inclusion, safeguarding, communications and visibility, financial procedures, MEAL (Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning) systems, and climate-smart agriculture. The session equipped staff with practical tools for effective grassroots implementation.

The mission concluded with a shared commitment to enhance information sharing and conduct periodic joint reviews. Key next steps include updating implementation plans based on stakeholder feedback, strengthening collaboration with relevant government ministries, and commencing project activities promptly to ensure timely delivery and measurable impact across targeted communities in Adamawa and Borno States.

June 2025

DG-ECHO Project I - Close Out Meeting with Borno State Stakeholders
Maiduguri, Borno State, Nigeria

The project “Providing Access to Safe Quality Education for Vulnerable Conflict-Affected Children in Borno State” was a two-year education initiative implemented by Jesuit Refugee Service (JRS) Nigeria in partnership with CATAI from July 2023 to June 2025. Funded by ECHO and co-funded by JM Nuremberg, the project aimed to restore safe, inclusive, and continuous learning opportunities for children affected by the prolonged crisis in Northeast Nigeria. Implementation focused on Dikwa and Monguno Local Government Areas, targeting internally displaced children, host community members, and out-of-school learners.

During the Close Out meeting, I represented the Country Program Unit to present the project interventions and major achievements. The project adopted a holistic and community-driven approach to education by integrating school rehabilitation, WASH infrastructure improvements, teacher capacity strengthening, menstrual hygiene support for adolescent girls, livelihood support for caregivers, and community engagement through School-Based Management Committees (SBMCs). Capitation grants were also provided to schools to address priority needs identified in their School Development Plans.

The project reached 25,215 individuals and significantly improved access to quality education. Key achievements included the renovation of 26 classrooms, construction of 25 gender-segregated latrine blocks, installation of 4 water reticulation systems, and 16 handwashing stations to improve school hygiene. Additionally, 391 classroom benches were distributed and 6 creches were established to support early childhood learning.

In response, JRS Nigeria, with support from Irish Jesuit International (IJI) through Misean Cara, conducted distributions of food items, non-food items, and hygiene kits to displaced households in Mokwa in August 2025.

To improve learning access and retention, 8,456 learners received essential school materials, while 3,395 adolescent girls received Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits to support school attendance. Furthermore, 129 children were referred to specialized services including child protection, health, and mental health and psychosocial support.

Supporting Household Resilience

Recognizing the link between household income and school attendance, the project supported 100 vulnerable caregivers with livelihood training and start-up kits in areas such as tailoring, petty trading, and food processing. As a result, 153 children were retained in school due to improved financial stability within their households.

Environmental Sustainability

The project also promoted environmental sustainability by planting 80 trees across 8 schools, contributing to climate resilience, improved air quality, and the creation of greener and more child-friendly learning environments.

May 2025

Program Quality and Distribution Monitoring - DG-ECHO Project I
Monguno and Dikwa LGAs, Borno State, Nigeria

As Deputy Head of Programs, I travelled to Maiduguri, Borno State from 18–24 May 2025 to monitor the delivery of education and hygiene interventions under the ECHO-funded project supporting displaced communities in Monguno LGA.

Educational Materials & Furniture: I oversaw the distribution of desks, benches, teacher chairs and tables to three schools — Central Primary School, Kuya Primary School, and Central JSS in Monguno LGA, addressing severe overcrowding and improving classroom organisation. Instructional materials including attendance registers, lesson notes, chalk, pens, and learning charts were distributed across all four project schools, including Gana Ali Primary School.

Crèche Installation: As part of efforts to improve attendance and retention, particularly for mothers and caregivers, I monitored the installation of fully equipped crèches at three schools. Each crèche was furnished with play materials, hygiene supplies, solar-powered fans, and baby furniture, creating safe, nurturing spaces for young children while their mothers attend school.

Classroom Renovation & WASH Infrastructure: I participated in the formal handover of renovated classrooms and newly constructed toilet facilities across four schools. A total of four blocks of two classrooms each were rehabilitated, alongside gender-segregated latrines and solar-powered lighting. The new facilities promote safer learning environments, encourage school retention for girls, and improve overall WASH standards in schools.

Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM): I monitored the distribution of 173 MHM kits to adolescent girls across the four schools. Each kit contained reusable sanitary pads, hygiene items, underwear, and washing materials. Distribution was accompanied by awareness sessions and hands-on demonstrations on menstrual hygiene practices, directly addressing harmful practices identified in the community.

Challenges & Lessons Learned: Logistical constraints, including limited truck availability caused delays in item delivery, while inadequate crowd control during one distribution resulted in the loss of 29 MHM kits. I documented these challenges and recommended improved logistics coordination and stronger stakeholder engagement with school staff to ensure safe and orderly distributions in future activities.

March 2025

Program Quality Assessment and Project Implementation Performance Review Meeting - BMZ/GIZ Project
Numan and Demsa LGAs, Adamawa State, Nigeria

As Deputy Head of Programs, I led the Country Office Team to Yola, Adamawa State from 9–13 March 2025 to lead the quarterly review of the GIZ-funded Gender Project, bringing together JRS (Adamawa and Abuja offices) and implementing partners Restoration of Hope (ROHI) and Jamil Alhakeem Foundation (JHF) for a structured three-day review process.

I facilitated an internal JRS team session to review field activities, consolidate lessons from quarter one, and prepare for partner engagement. A key agenda item was the reassessment of the Direct Implementation Plan (DIP) following a delayed project start, requiring adjustments to timelines, strategies, and the activity budget to reflect actual implementation realities.

All partners, together with GIZ representatives for a comprehensive review. I coordinated presentations on project achievements, challenges, and financial performance — including the identification of budget gaps and the need for reallocation across budget lines. Practical solutions were discussed, such as clustering field activities to reduce staff workload and providing transport reimbursements to beneficiaries to improve participation.

I facilitated presentations from ROHI and JHF, capturing their implementation updates, field challenges, and lessons learned. A critical concern raised was the absence of stipends for field volunteers and limited transport support for staff. The session concluded with structured quarter two planning, ensuring realistic and achievable targets going forward.

Key Outcomes & Lessons Learned: The review yielded several important insights: including spouses of Community Development Leaders (CDLs) in training sessions had strengthened community buy-in; many target communities had never previously received any form of intervention, underscoring the project's significance; and community leaders consistently demonstrated strong commitment and availability. Agreed actions included budget reallocation, safeguarding consent processes for child-focused holiday activities, and timely documentation for fund disbursements.

Strategic Recommendations: Drawing from the review, I put forward recommendations including the introduction of a livelihood component to the gender project, co-creation of budgets with partners, private sector and philanthropist engagement, greater community and government leadership of project activities, and the establishment of school-level safeguarding action plans.

July 2024 to January 2025

Assessment of Impacts of Oil Contamination in South-South Nigeria on Livelihoods of Households
Geospatial Analysis using GIS, Soil and Water Quality Test, and Economic Loss Analysis

The Niger Delta region of Nigeria, located along the Gulf of Guinea, is rich in petroleum resources but suffers from severe environmental pollution, often described as ecocide. Oil spills caused by multinational corporations are frequent, leading to widespread contamination of land and water. Gas flaring further worsens air quality, filling communities with soot and toxic fumes. The environmental degradation has devastated the livelihoods of residents, who primarily depend on fishing and farming. The region, home to Africa’s largest mangrove forests, faces land grabbing, human rights abuses, and sociocultural disruptions.

An assessment by Jesuit Refugee Service (JRS) and Home of Mother Earth Foundation (HOMEF) examined oil contamination in farmlands in Akwa Ibom and Rivers States. The findings highlighted the extensive damage to agricultural production and the economic instability faced by affected communities, particularly in Okoro Utip and Eteo, where pollution has disrupted livelihoods.

Okoro Utip, established in the 18th century by the Ibeno people of the Obolo (Andoni) ethnic group, is a fishing community in Akwa Ibom State, located in a mangrove swamp forest along the Atlantic coastline. The area is lowlying and prone to flooding, with an estuary that discharges into the ocean. The community, with an estimated population of over 20,000, relies on fishing, farming, and petty trading. It experiences year-round rainfall, peaking between May and September, supporting its agricultural and fishing activities. Infrastructure is limited, with a single major road under construction to connect it to the EastWest Road.

Eteo, in Rivers State, is a farming and fishing community split by the EastWest Road. It has one main road and several earth roads, with a minor road leading to the Primary Health Center. The community depends on three interconnected streams for freshwater, but these have been frequently polluted due to oil pipelines and sand mining activities. Heavy seasonal rainfall from March to November, peaking in July, makes the environment favorable for farming and fishing.

Both communities face environmental challenges, particularly from oil spills and pollution, threatening their livelihoods and natural resources. In Okoro Utip, Akwa Ibom State, residents experienced multiple oil spills in 2023 and 2024, originating from Mobil Producing Nigeria Unlimited offshore facility. The spills, carried by ocean tides, flood the lowlying community and contaminate farmlands, especially during the rainy season. No cleanup or remediation efforts were made. In Eteo, Rivers State, two oil spills in 2023 resulted from a ruptured Nigerian National Petroleum Company (NNPC) pipeline. The crude oil contaminated nearby streams and farmlands, yet no proper investigation or response was conducted. Residents felt oppressed as NNPC secured the site with armed forces but took no action to clean up the spill. These two cases represent a fraction of the widespread oil pollution in the Niger Delta, where spills devastate farmlands and water sources, often without any cleanup, restoration, or reparation efforts.

The study highlights the urgent need to address the ongoing environmental and socioeconomic impacts of oil and gas activities in the region.

The farmlands in Eteo and Okoro Utip have been severely impacted by oil spills from local oil company activities, leading to soil and water contamination. Soil analysis in Eteo and Okoro Utip confirmed a high degree of contamination (CD ≥ 24) and a pollution load index (PLI > 1), indicating severe pollution. The contamination disrupts microbial communities, affects nutrient cycling, and poses risks to groundwater quality. Polluted sediments have further degraded water quality and harmed aquatic ecosystems.

Water quality tests revealed that both surface and groundwater are more acidic than WHO recommended levels (pH 6.5–8.5), making them unsuitable for drinking. While Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) and Electrical Conductivity (EC) remain within WHO limits in most cases, polluted surface water showed high contamination (EC =15,834 μS/cm). Dissolved Oxygen (DO) levels indicate minimal contaminant transmission between surface and groundwater. Nitrate levels, although currently within safe limits, pose future risks of leaching. Heavy metal contamination is evident, with lead exceeding WHO limits (0.01 mg/L) in both surface and groundwater, posing potential health risks. Copper and zinc levels remain low, while nickel, chromium, and manganese are within safety thresholds. In Okoro Utip, vanadium, nickel, and chromium concentrations are higher in groundwater than in surface water, though still within acceptable limits.

The Water Quality Index (WQI) for Eteo rates surface water as “grade E” (unsuitable for drinking) and borehole water as “grade B” (good quality). In Okoro Utip, surface water is “grade A” (excellent), but borehole water varies from “grade B” (good) to “grade D” (very poor).

Polluted water sources in Eteo (pH 5.3) and Okoro Utip (pH 6.7) spread pollutants across farmlands, worsening contamination. In Eteo, an oil spill within 50 meters of the Oken Alebo stream led to widespread contamination, particularly during the rainy season. In Okoro Utip, high tides carried crude oil inland, polluting creeks, mangroves, and farmlands.

The extent of farmland loss was severe:
  • Eteo: 59 farmlands assessed, covering 56.14 hectares. Oil spills contaminated 21.85 hectares, leading to total agricultural loss.
  • Okoro Utip: 37 farmlands assessed, covering 5.34 hectares, all affected by oil pollution.
The estimated economic loss due to reduced productivity is:
  • NGN 4,491,764.09 for Okoro Utip farmers
  • NGN 34,218,182.71 for Eteo farmers

Due to the impacts of oil contamination on farmlands, farmers in those communities struggle to sustain their families, with some considering leaving their ancestral lands in search of alternative livelihoods, increasing the risk of rural-urban migration and economic instability.

Sociocultural Impacts

The loss of productive farmland has disrupted traditional farming knowledge, as younger generations lose interest in agriculture. Social tensions have emerged over land use, fragmenting community cohesion. Farmers face rising costs for fertilizers and soil rehabilitation, pushing many further into poverty and debt. Beyond financial strain, the crisis has taken an emotional toll, with rising stress, depression, and anxiety, particularly among women, who play a key role in farming and household sustenance.

December 2024

DG-ECHO Phase I - Semi-Annual Project Performance Review Meeting and FCM Training for Project Staff
Maiduguri, Borno State, Nigeria

As Deputy Head of Programs, I travelled to Maiduguri and Monguno LGAs, Borno State from 18–21 December 2024 to lead the ECHO project's mid-point performance review and deliver accountability training to field staff.

Quarterly Performance Review: Over two days, I convened the full project team for a comprehensive review of progress against targets, examining milestones achieved, challenges encountered, and lessons learned across the project's first phase. The review created space for open, collaborative problem-solving and resulted in a detailed implementation plan for the final six months — outlining specific activities, timelines, and individual responsibilities to ensure a focused and coordinated push toward project completion.

Accountability & MEAL Training: Working alongside the Monitoring and Evaluation Officer, I led a two-day accountability training for field staff covering data management, accurate and timely reporting, transparent financial management, and stakeholder engagement. The session combined presentations, group discussions, and interactive exercises to build practical skills. A key focus was equipping staff to collect, analyse, and apply data effectively — and to understand the direct link between strong accountability practices and donor confidence, programme credibility, and community trust. Staff feedback was overwhelmingly positive, with new reporting tools and templates introduced to improve data quality going forward.

Key Recommendations: The mission concluded with actionable recommendations including closer collaboration with the project team to secure all deliverables, accelerating expenditure to improve burn rates, institutionalising quarterly review meetings, and ensuring all project school environments are assessed and maintained at optimal functional standards.

October 2024

DG-ECHO Monitoring Visit to JRS Project Sites
Monguno LGA, Borno State, Nigeria

Representing JRS Nigeria Operations, I travelled to Maiduguri and Monguno LGA, Borno State from 12–16 November 2024 to support and facilitate the ECHO donor monitoring visit to JRS project implementation sites; a key accountability exercise assessing project impact on displaced communities.

Facilitating the Donor Visit, I coordinated the monitoring visit for Mr. Aro Olaleye, the ECHO representative from Abuja, guiding the team across JRS project sites in Monguno. The visit focused on gathering direct feedback from beneficiaries, school management, and government authorities on implementation quality, community benefits, and areas for improvement.

At Central JSS, the ECHO representative engaged school leadership, the School-Based Management Committee (SBMC), and students including members of the Adolescent Girls' Club - who demonstrated clear learning outcomes in hygiene management and awareness of violence against girls. The school shared comprehensive evidence of JRS's support: teacher training, increased enrolment, renovation of a burned classroom block, provision of recreational materials through capitation grants, and plans for a mini library. Challenges noted included a shortage of specialist teachers and reduced staffing capacity due to ongoing capacity-building training in Bama LGA.

At Gana Ali Primary School, the team met with school leadership to review enrolment trends and infrastructure needs. Enrolment had increased significantly, driven by JRS's implementation and the high concentration of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in the area. The visit also highlighted JRS's notable initiative of enrolling Alternative Learning Programme (ALP) graduates, certified by the Borno State Agency for Mass Education into formal schools, which the ECHO representative commended as an exemplary collaboration with a state agency.

Engagement with the Local Government Education Authority (LGEA): The monitoring team's meeting with LGEA officials provided strong validation of JRS's impact at the systems level. Officials reported a 35% reduction in out-of-school children in Monguno LGA, improved teaching quality through JRS-supported teacher training, joint monitoring initiatives, and broader community benefits through Income-Generating Activities (IGA) for parents. LGEA officials passionately advocated for increased funding to enable JRS to scale up its work across the LGA.

Recommendations: The visit identified infrastructure maintenance needs including water reticulation improvements, borehole maintenance, repairs to a flood-damaged ramp at Central JSS, and fixing a leaking storage tank — all flagged for follow-up action with community stakeholders and the LGEA.

March 2024

Project Endline Evaluation - USAID/IOM Rapid Response Fund (RRF) Project
Damasak, Mobbar LGA, Borno State, Nigeria

As part of JRS Nigeria's emergency response to the humanitarian crisis in Damasak, driven by conflict-induced displacement, overcrowding, and a large influx of returnees from Niger Republic; I led the conduct of an endline survey was conducted to assess the impact of Protection and WASH interventions delivered under the Rapid Response Fund (RRF) project across three sites: GSS IDP Camp, Damasak Central, and Zanna Umarti communities.

Methodology: I coordinated the engagement and training of 9 enumerators in remote data collection methodologies using hand-held devices. Questionnaires were developed and administered through the ODK platform (KoboCollect), enabling efficient and accurate data collection directly from project beneficiaries. This approach was designed to generate an in-depth understanding of project outcomes and real-world impact at the beneficiary level.

Survey Reach & Profile: The survey captured responses from 616 participants, the overwhelming majority of whom were adults (98.86%) and household decision-makers (93.51%), providing a reliable and representative picture of project impact at the household level.

Impact on Quality of Life: Nearly all respondents — 98.86% — reported that JRS's RRF interventions improved their quality of life. Beneficiaries attributed this to improved access to potable water and sanitation facilities, better health outcomes, and an enhanced sense of safety resulting from protection awareness campaigns and case management support.

Access to Lifesaving Services: 98.54% of respondents confirmed improved access to lifesaving NFIs, shelter, WASH facilities, and protection services — demonstrating broad and effective coverage across the intervention areas.

Accountability & Dignity: The survey consistently reflected high standards of accountability and dignified service delivery across all measured principles. Over 98% of respondents reported feeling safe accessing services, being treated with respect by JRS staff and volunteers, feeling well-informed, and having their views and complaints heard and acted upon — affirming JRS's commitment to a safe, participatory, and accountable humanitarian response.

Recommendations: The survey findings underscored the ongoing need for humanitarian assistance in Damasak, with specific recommendations to increase the quantity of items within hygiene kits, expand beneficiary coverage to reach additional IDP households, and sustain programming to address the continuing displacement crisis.
