August 2026

Project Inception Meeting - Funded by Caritas Canada
Okoro Utip Community, Ibeno LGA, Akwa Ibom State

On behalf of JRS Nigeria, I led the CARITAS Canada-Funded Project inception meeting in Akwa Ibom State on 6th August 2026. This marked the official launch of a new project titled "Strengthening Community Resilience to Oil Spill Impacts in Akwa Ibom State." It welcomed participants from a wide range of relevant stakeholders from the State Government (Ministry of Lands), Government Agencies (NESREA, NOSDRA), Local Government (PHC Coordinator), Non-Governmental/Civil Society Organisations (JDPC, Policy Alert, PPDF, NAPPDRR, LEARN), the Media (Arise News, Premium Times), and the Okoro Utip Community.

The project seeks to strengthen the resilience of climate-vulnerable members of Okoro Utip Community in Ibeno Local Government Area of Akwa Ibom State by promoting meaningful community participation in addressing the environmental, social, and economic impacts of oil contamination.

Drawing on the African tradition of communal dialogue, commonly referred to as the African Palaver approach, the initiative will create safe and inclusive spaces where community members can openly share their lived experiences, build collective understanding of environmental challenges, and identify locally driven solutions. Rather than replacing the responsibilities of government, the project is designed to foster constructive collaboration among communities, government institutions, civil society organizations, traditional institutions, and other stakeholders in advancing ecological justice, environmental restoration, and sustainable development.

I had the opportunity to make presentations about JRS, the report of the assessment conducted, and the overview of the project (Goal, results, activities and expectations). Participants engaged in series of reflections, feedback and goodwill messages while also commending the project in the light of appealing towards an improved scope and expansion to other coastal communities within and outside Akwa Ibom State.

Through awareness creation, community advocacy, and community-based environmental monitoring, the project will empower residents of Okoro Utip to actively participate in decisions affecting their environment while strengthening local capacities to respond to the long-term impacts of oil pollution.

Project Performance Review Meeting/Data Quality Assessment
DG ECHO Funded Project, Borno State - North East Nigeria.

Sectoral Achievements: The review highlighted strong results across core program areas. Education reached 6,207 formal learners and 4,970 out-of-school children through the Accelerated Learning Program, rehabilitated 8 classroom blocks and 24 latrines across 15 schools, and trained 292 teachers and 149 SBMC members. Protection and MHPSS efforts identified 27 protection cases, reached 8,350 people through hygiene promotion, and distributed 4,000 hygiene kits, while enrolling 360 Boys & Girls Club members. The Livelihood component assessed nearly 5,900 households, trained 450 IGA beneficiaries, formed 18 VSLAs, and distributed 150 start-up kits, though limited funding required grouping beneficiaries into cooperatives.

Quality, Compliance & Finance: We focused on field quality and donor compliance, following up on ECHO's June visit around head teacher ownership, WASH completion, renovation quality, and branding corrections, alongside a zero-tolerance stance on unverified delivery documentation. MEAL survey data showed strong outcomes, including 98.4% hygiene awareness and 97% rating service delivery as safe and dignified. Financially, 42% of the total budget had been spent, together with the Implementing Partner.

The DQA leveraged on the RDQA tool which identified KPIs, validating them from Primary Document Source (Paper) to Secondary Document Source (MIS - ActivityInfo), and triangulating them with the reported data. Key areas of gaps were identified and recommendations for strengthening also provided.

13 - 19 July 2026

Programme Quality Control and Assurance - Education Infrastructure
Dikwa and Monguno LGAs, Borno State - North East Nigeria

As Deputy Head of Programs, I led a field monitoring visit to infrastructure renovation and WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) works at public schools within Dikwa and Monguno LGAs in Borno State as part of an Education in Emergencies (EiE) program funded by DG ECHO and implemented by JRS (Jesuit Refugee Service).

The renovations included a total of 4 blocks of 10 classrooms (a total of 2 blocks with 4 classrooms in Monguno LGA, a total of 2 blocks with 6 classrooms in Dikwa LGA) were assessed for quality control and assurance. This was intended to ensure they are restored to minimum standards for safe learning.

Key findings — construction quality issues (common across sites):

  • Doors and door frames breaking away from walls
  • Poor, uneven, or unfinished painting
  • Broken windows
  • Leaking ceilings/roofs, bad ceiling work, and broken floors
  • Poorly plastered walls with visible holes
  • Low-quality desks and chairs, already showing breakage
  • Substandard ramps posing accessibility barriers

WASH-specific issues:

  • Water reticulation incomplete or not yet done at multiple sites
  • Existing water supply had low pressure and was non-potable
  • Stagnant water pooling in compounds, creating vector-breeding risk
  • Toilets built as pour-flush systems without connected water
  • A newly renovated toilet roof failed just three days before the visit

    • Recommendations:

    • Remedial action: The contractor should return to Monguno and Dikwa to correct substandard work, to protect the organization's reputation and match quality standards of peer organizations.
    • Technical scoping: Bills of Quantities (BoQs) used for procurement should be developed by qualified technical specialists in collaboration with the Country Program Team, aligned with global EiE standards.
    • Bid evaluation rigor: The tender committees should validate BoQs against real costs to guard against predatory (artificially low) pricing, and include technical (not just administrative/financial) evaluation for specialized bids covering construction, water supply, sanitation, and temporary learning spaces.
    • Quality assurance: Ongoing QA/QC should be conducted by the Country Program Team or delegated specialists, with formal validation required before final payment tranches are released to contractors, given field staff's limited technical construction/WASH expertise.

    January 2026

    Digital Learning - The Ideas Cube Box
    Kuchingoro IDP Camp, Games Village - Abuja, Nigeria

    To lead step-down trainings and facilitation of Digital Learnings using Information Technology Devices, I led multiple sessions for staff, volunteers and practical sessions for the displaced population in an IDP Camp, Kuchigoro - Abuja.

    The Ideas Cube is a compact, 100% autonomous nano-server developed by BSF. It creates a local Wi-Fi hotspot — no internet connection or electricity required — allowing up to 20 users to simultaneously connect via smartphone, tablet, or computer and access preloaded educational and cultural content. Built from 100% recycled plastic with easily replaceable parts, it offers up to 6 hours of battery life and storage of up to 1TB.

    OLIP is the digital library platform hosted on the Ideas Cube. It organises content into themes (like library corridors), playlists (like shelves), and applications — covering topics such as health, mental wellbeing, literacy, arts, GBV awareness, vocational skills, and more. Users can browse, watch, read, and download content in visitor mode without needing a password, while Administrators and Facilitators can customise the homepage, manage users, and monitor activity.

    Kajou Cards are supplementary content cards that can be installed on the Ideas Cube either online or offline, expanding the library's resources without requiring a permanent internet connection. Sessions included capacity-building session on Conflict-Related Sexual Violence (CRSV) and training on the use of Kajou Cards, an e-library resource that supports facilitators in delivering awareness and learning sessions.

    Conflict-Related Sexual Violence (CRSV) is a serious protection concern affecting displaced populations such as internally displaced persons (IDPs), refugees, and asylum seekers. It includes acts like rape, sexual exploitation, forced marriage, and other forms of abuse linked to conflict and insecurity. Displacement often increases vulnerability due to family separation, weak protection systems, unsafe living conditions in camps or informal settlements, and reliance on humanitarian assistance for survival. Reporting of incidences (Survivor or third party) were encouraged through a secured platform for necessary actions.

    Overall, the guide equips facilitators with the skills to conduct structured, community-centred sessions using the Ideas Cube — from preparing activities and managing group dynamics to programming content that meets community needs. Participants will understand content creation, video creations, life skills through pre-recorded lessons using android devices and other IT equipment.

    October 2025

    Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) Exercise
    Mokwa, Niger State, Nigeria

    Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) is an essential step in humanitarian assistance that takes place after aid such as cash, food, or relief items has been delivered to affected populations. It helps organisations understand whether assistance reached the intended beneficiaries, met their priority needs, and was provided in a safe, fair, and dignified manner.

    About 2 months after the distribution, I facilitated a session of training and capacity building for community-based enumerators to conduct CAPI/assessment using for the beneficiairy households. PDM strengthens accountability to communities by creating opportunities for beneficiaries to share feedback on the relevance and quality of support received. It also helps identify protection concerns, gaps in targeting, and any unintended negative effects of assistance. This ensures that programmes remain responsive to the realities faced by vulnerable populations, including displaced communities.

    The findings from the Post Distribution Monitoring were used to improve programme design and implementation. We were able to adjust distribution methods, enhance communication with communities, strengthen complaint and feedback mechanisms, and ensured that future interventions were more efficient and impactful.

    Beyond verification, the PDM provided a continuous learning opportunity. Lessons gathered helped us as humanitarian actors to better understand community preferences, market conditions, and operational challenges. This enables the delivery of more timely, appropriate, and dignified assistance while strengthening transparency and trust with both affected populations and donors.

    October 2025

    Retrospective Baseline and Midline Assessment for YAPPI
    Youth in Animation and Post Production Initiative

    Nigeria’s creative industry holds immense potential to drive economic growth and youth empowerment. It has emerged as a critical driver of inclusive growth, innovation, and youth empowerment. However, despite its potential to diversify the national economy and reduce unemployment, the sector continues to face systemic challenges that limit its competitiveness and inclusivity, particularly in urban centers such as Lagos, Enugu, and the Federal Capital Territory (FCT).

    Nigeria’s youth unemployment rate remains among the highest globally, estimated at approximately 53%, affecting more than 80 million young people aged 15–35. This economic fragility constrains domestic demand for creative services and limits the ability of young creatives to sustain careers. Moreover, the creative sector’s labor market is characterized by informality and underemployment, with limited social protection for workers.

    The Youth in Animation and Post-Production Initiative (YAPPI) was designed to address these gaps by targeting and training youth (aged 18–35, 75% female, 5% PWD) across 3 States of Lagos, Enugu, and FCT, Nigeria. The aim of the project is to equip youths with advanced creative skills in animation & post-production through a comprehensive curriculum, market readiness training, and mentorship programs.

    The Youth in Animation and Post-Production Initiative (YAPPI) stands as a transformative force in Nigeria’s creative economy, empowering 60,000 youth 80% female and 5% persons with disabilities (PWD) with advanced animation and post-production skills to drive employability and entrepreneurship.

    At the midpoint of this 5-year initiative, the midline assessment is a critical Monitoring, Evaluation, Research, and Learning (MERL) endeavour to evaluate progress, validate the program’s Theory of Change (ToC), and inform strategic adaptations.

    I anchored on the OECD-DAC evaluation criteria (Relevance, Coherence, Effectiveness, Efficiency, Impact, Sustainability), this assessment will deliver rigorous, evidence-based insights to enhance YAPPI’s impact.

    By prioritizing inclusivity, localization, and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, the assessment ensures that female youth and PWD are central to the evaluation process, fostering equitable outcomes in Nigeria’s creative industry.
